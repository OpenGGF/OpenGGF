# OpenGGF 0.7 — Release Summary

> **Status:** 0.7.20261105, released 2026-11-05. Shipped re-scoped: the
> roadmap's complete Sonic 3 & Knuckles campaigns, full-run trace chains and
> whole-campaign qualification were not met and move to 0.8 as accepted limitations
> (see [Known limitations and release status](#known-limitations-and-release-status)).

OpenGGF 0.7 is a campaign-breadth and platform release for the open-source Java
engine that recreates the main Sonic the Hedgehog games from user-supplied ROM
images. Sonic 3 & Knuckles grows from 0.6's Angel Island → Hydrocity slice into a
broad playable implementation of the locked-on game, through to its native
endings and credits. Sonic 1 and Sonic 2 keep their complete flows and gain more
native corrections, and Knuckles in Sonic 2 arrives. Around the games, 0.7 adds a
new title hub and menus, supported widescreen, region-timed frame pacing, a
level editor with named projects, OpenGGF Stageplay, a rewind budget and the
bundled Time Attack mod. The mod framework is included as a preview; its API
is unpublished and publishes in 0.8.

Release artifacts are built on GitHub-hosted Windows, macOS and Linux runners,
after hosted ordinary tests and a separate structural-guard session. Those
machines do not have the private ROM fixtures, so ROM-backed validation remains
separate release evidence.

## Highlights

### Sonic 3 & Knuckles: from a vertical slice to the whole map

S3K is the largest area of the release. 0.6 shipped Angel Island → Hydrocity as
its release route; 0.7 implements gameplay and progression through every zone of
the locked-on game. It is a **beta**: playable much further than before, with
known route and parity gaps.

- **Every zone has a playable implementation.** Marble Garden, Carnival Night,
  IceCap, Launch Base, Mushroom Hill, Flying Battery, Sandopolis, Lava Reef,
  Hidden Palace, Sky Sanctuary, Death Egg and The Doomsday Zone gain their
  traversal objects, badniks, minibosses, bosses, events, scroll, palette and
  animated art, ported from the disassembly. Hidden Palace's data-select slot now
  enters the playable act (`$1601`) rather than the Super Emerald sanctuary.
- **The late game.** Sky Sanctuary's three boss fights ending with Mecha Sonic,
  Death Egg Act 2's reversing gravity, and a playable Doomsday Zone: Sonic falls
  in, transforms (Hyper with all Super Emeralds) and flies the asteroid field
  through both boss phases.
- **Native endings for all three characters.** Sonic and Tails continue from
  Death Egg or Doomsday into the Tornado rescue, earned Super Emerald restoration,
  ROM credits and their progress-dependent final scenes; Knuckles continues from
  the final Sky Sanctuary fight through his plane flight, island credits and
  terminal scene, then hands over to the title screen. These stay inside the
  native level lifecycle rather than a separate ending mode.
- **Characters and routes.** Knuckles breaks the Knuckles-only walls in mid-air,
  glides and climbs under reversed gravity, and has his own route content;
  Tails alone enters Carnival Night and Mushroom Hill with the native fly-in.
  Super and Hyper forms, elemental shields and the Insta-Shield follow the ROM's
  timing through stage returns and rewind.
- **Stages and saves.** Blue Sphere, all three bonus-stage families and the
  Super Emerald shrine get native presentation and return handling. Saves retain
  collected giant rings and the Chaos Special Stage rotation, and resume stock
  zones at their native starting act.
- **Bounded campaign coverage.** Preserved cold inputs now carry Sonic alone
  uninterrupted from Lava Reef act 1 into Death Egg, beside the Sonic and Tails,
  Tails-alone and Knuckles route replays, and a cold 800-pixel Sonic route covers
  both Death Egg acts and the ordinary ending. These are route evidence, not a
  certified complete campaign (see the limitations below).

The [game status guide](../guide/playing/game-status.md#sonic-3--knuckles-s3k)
and the [native ending matrix](../architecture/validation/levels/s3k-ending.md)
separate what is implemented from what is verified.

### Sonic 1 and Sonic 2

- Both games remain playable from start to finish, including bosses, special
  stages, endings and credits, with further corrections to object slots, level
  entry, background scrolling, sidekick timing and results bonuses.
- **Original revisions.** Sonic 1 and Sonic 2 recognise their original REV00
  images as well as REV01, and select the matching ROM data and shipped
  behaviour. Unknown images keep the existing unverified compatibility path.
- **Knuckles in Sonic 2.** Choosing Knuckles in Sonic 2 activates a built-in
  patch implemented from the s2disasm `knuckles-in-sonic-2` branch: KiS2 physics,
  glide and climb, and the rewritten object layouts. It needs the S3K image and
  the Sonic 2 ROM; with a user-supplied S&K + Sonic 2 lock-on dump it also reads
  the lock-on chip's own art, palettes, title intro and Super Knuckles data.
- **Sonic 2 retail options.** The title opens the original options screen
  (Sonic and Tails, Sonic alone or Tails alone, and the sound test).
- Enemy chains award 10,000 points from the sixteenth consecutive defeat, as in
  the native games.

### Title hub, menus, settings and languages

- **Master title hub.** A cartridge shelf shows each game's original animated
  ROM logo, names missing ROMs on their card, and shows what Start will launch.
  **Game Setup** holds per-game choices; **Settings** becomes tabs (Display,
  Sound, Controls, Gameplay, Files, Capture) that apply to all games.
- **ROMs found by contents.** The engine recognises each user-supplied image by
  size and cartridge header rather than file name, accepts lock-on dumps, and
  can boot S3K from separate Sonic 3 and Sonic & Knuckles images.
- **Display modes.** Windowed, borderless and fullscreen, with integer or
  aspect-preserving scaling; window-mode changes revert after 15 seconds unless
  confirmed. Holding Escape shrinks a paused game into its card.
- **Interface languages.** Menus, settings and the editor use contributor-editable
  catalogues with 20 initial locale choices. Translations are machine drafts and
  marked as such; legal text falls back to English.

### Widescreen and frame pacing

- **Supported widescreen.** UI, HUD, titles, results, endings, all three special
  stages and capture render at 352x224 and 400x224 as supported targets, with
  528x224 best-effort and 800x224 exploratory. Native 320x224 remains the gameplay
  and trace authority, and pixels beyond native camera bounds are masked
  automatically.
- **Region-timed simulation.** Live simulation and audio run at 60 Hz NTSC or
  50 Hz PAL independently of the window frame-rate limit. With the default
  `display.pacing: AUTO`, a display at a whole multiple of the region rate locks
  one update per refresh, removing the periodic repeated picture on 59.94 Hz and
  60.02 Hz panels.
- **Optional interpolation.** `display.interpolation` (off by default) smooths
  camera, players and objects on higher-refresh displays. It is render-only and
  adds up to one tick of visual latency; recordings, screenshots and traces stay
  exact. Vendor variable-refresh behaviour is unqualified.

### Rewind, recording and capture

- **Rewind budget.** Live rewind now has a consumable budget, 10 seconds by
  default, refilled per checkpoint, act or zone (or unlimited), with an optional
  life-loss refill and separate bonus/special-stage allowances.
- Rewind restores children created by live objects, and its verification compares
  captured state by content.
- Recording keeps one control tick and blocks rewind while recording; capture
  buffers final PCM so video and audio stay in step, and `GameplayCaptureTool`
  pictures or films any gameplay section from the production boot path.

### Audio

- Each frame advances the sound chips by exactly one NTSC hardware frame (3,420 x
  262 master clocks) instead of 1/60 s of chip time.
- Music ownership follows the ROMs through invincibility, drowning, bosses and
  S3K cutscene fades; Sonic 1 percussion plays on direct launches, and the Sonic 2
  title star releases its sound-effect priority as the ROM does.
- An opt-in diagnostic output feeds the accurate FM core through a blip_buf port
  for comparison with BizHawk; on an identical write schedule it matches Genesis
  Plus GX source vectors byte for byte. This is a comparison tool, not a claim of
  chip-level parity in normal play.

### Level editor

The experimental level editor now works with **named projects** that create,
edit and reopen complete authored levels for Sonic 1, Sonic 2 and Sonic 3 & Knuckles
from ROM-derived asset libraries, with atomic saves, playtests that return to the
editor with undo history, and host-validated exports (S1/S2 v1 and S3K v2). Route
tapes open in both the in-engine editor and the separate OpenGGF Editor desktop app.

### OpenGGF Stageplay

New in 0.7, Stageplay directs short films and mod cutscenes with the games' own
characters and physics. Scenes are authored in the in-engine workspace, the
OpenGGF Editor or from screenplays: actor movement and facing, poses, camera
moves and zoom, captions, ROM audio and imported voices, and cuts or wipes across
locations, acts and games. Scenes export to video with FFmpeg or play inside
mods. Stageplay is **experimental**; its format is not frozen. See the
[Stageplay guide](../guide/creating/stageplay.md).

### Time Attack and online racing

Solo ghosts, retries and LAN/master-server racing ship together as the bundled
Time Attack mod, enabled by default and launched from **Extras > Time Attack**.
Rooms gain a playable lobby level, chat, multiple attempts per round and
TLS-protected remote endpoints. Network services are qualified further in 0.8.

### Mod framework preview

0.7 includes the mod framework that 0.8 will publish: patch, code, art and music
mods, mod scenes, characters, standalone games and custom zones, with a Mod
Manager, creator SDK and the `ggfmod` tool. Native packages for Linux x64,
Windows x64 and Apple Silicon can load code mods through pinned GraalVM Community
runtime class loading, which is an experimental facility. Mods may use any licence
under the OpenGGF Mod Exception.

The **Mod API is an unpublished candidate** in 0.7: its surface can change before
0.8 and carries no compatibility promise. The example mods in `examples/` (among
them Emerald Remix, Sitar Hero, Putt Putt Paradise, Typing of the Hedgehog and
Super Sonic Bros.) are examples of the framework, not released products.

### Build and packaging

- Java 25 is the baseline for source builds, the engine, the creator SDK and the
  examples; release packages use GraalVM 25 native images, with Intel macOS on the
  JVM distribution.

The complete list of changes is in [CHANGELOG.0.7.md](../../CHANGELOG.0.7.md).

## Validation status

Measured on 2026-11-05 at release candidate `e7aafca11` (Java 25, display-free
surfaceless EGL, original ROM dumps found by SHA-1). Later commits change only CI
workflows, the branch-policy scripts and documentation; the full suite, guards and
route pilot were repeated on the published commit before it was pushed.

- **Full ordinary suite** (`python3 tools/testing/maven_queue.py -Dmse=off -Pfull-suite test -B`):
  27,386 tests, 1 failure, 0 errors, 39 skips; every skip is allowed by
  `tools/testing/release-skip-policy.json`. The failure,
  `TestGolfScenePresentation.locallyComposedSceneMatchesTheProductionFramebuffer[1]`
  (Putt Putt Paradise example mod), is order-dependent: the class passes 45/45 on
  its own on the candidate and on develop `f6e1ba2a4`, and it failed the same way
  on an earlier candidate whose code equalled develop. It needs the Sonic 2 ROM, so
  the hosted release build skips it. It is tracked for 0.8, not a 0.7 regression.
- **Structural guards** (`python3 tools/testing/maven_queue.py -Dmse=off -Pguards test -B`):
  834 tests, 0 failures, 0 errors, 0 skips.
- **Local ROM route pilot** (the bounded AIZ1/HCZ1 pilot described in
  [release publishing](../project/release-publishing.md#local-exact-candidate-rom-qualification)):
  `check_route_obligations.py --run --expected-candidate` passed all 26 exact
  cases with zero skips at the candidate; a bounded route pilot, not release
  certification.

Trace replay is held to the no-regression contract in
[trace-scope-release-7.md](../status/trace-scope-release-7.md): traces passing on
the baseline must keep passing, and known-red traces must not gain failures or
regress their frontier. It is not an all-green gate. For scale, the dated
2026-11-05 [native-frontiers qualification](../status/trace-frontier-log.md#2026-11-05--native-frontiers-lead-qualification)
measured the `trace-replay-r7` profile at 121 tests with 66 failures on
`a89b85806`. Run with that record's exact commands at `e7aafca11`, the `trace-replay`
(898 cases, 9 failing), `trace-replay-r7` and `trace-segments` (70 cases, 40
failing) profiles match it case by case: no trace gained a failure or changed its
first failure. Two GL-backed r7 cases need display-free EGL and pass with it; one
new Slots bonus segment case passes.

## Known limitations and release status

The maintainer chose to ship 0.7 now, re-scoped, rather than hold it for the
remaining roadmap gates. The following [0.7 roadmap](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md)
milestones are **not met**. They are accepted limitations of this release and
carry over to the [0.8 roadmap](../project/v0.8-roadmap.md):

- **Shared S3K campaign backbone** ([Milestone 1](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md#milestone-1--complete-the-shared-s3k-campaign-backbone)):
  partially landed. A clean save progressing through the whole campaign graph,
  with focused coverage of every transition, is not certified.
- **Knuckles complete route** ([Milestone 2](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md#milestone-2--knuckles-complete-route)):
  open. No clean AIZ-to-Mecha-Sonic playthrough with green trace segments is
  recorded.
- **Tails complete route** ([Milestone 3](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md#milestone-3--tails-complete-route)):
  open. No clean solo-Tails AIZ-to-DEZ playthrough with green trace segments is
  recorded.
- **Sonic complete route** ([Milestone 4](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md#milestone-4--sonic-complete-route)):
  open, for both the normal ending and the emerald-selected Doomsday branch.
- **All three outros** ([Milestone 5](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md#milestone-5--complete-all-three-outros)):
  the native endings are implemented with bounded lifecycle, rewind and selected
  pixel evidence. Reaching each one from a full input-driven route, and
  whole-scene visual and audio parity, are not established.
- **Full-run trace chains** ([Milestone 6](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md#milestone-6--complete-the-full-run-trace-chains)):
  none of the four continuous chains (Knuckles, Tails, Sonic normal, Sonic
  emerald) reaches its terminal boundary, and many constituent S3K segments still
  diverge. Score trace coverage alongside rings also remains open.
- **Whole-game qualification** ([Milestone 7](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md#milestone-7--qualify-complete-stock-games-and-retain-added-features)):
  input-driven complete-flow evidence for Sonic 1 and Sonic 2, the stock
  compatibility matrix and the required route and ending visuals are not all
  recorded. Region-timed simulation is implemented; vendor variable-refresh
  behaviour is unverified.
- **Release gate** ([Milestone 8](../project/v0.7-roadmap.md#milestone-8--release-07-and-establish-the-08-baseline)):
  its "all advertised stock-game outcomes complete" condition is replaced by this
  re-scoped release. 0.7 makes no complete-campaign claim, so 0.8 starts from
  an unfrozen S3K campaign baseline.

Other limitations in 0.7:

- **Parity is not certified.** Playable does not mean identical to the original
  everywhere: OpenGGF claims neither complete S3K campaigns nor full trace
  parity. Chip-level audio parity is not established; live FM-core switching is
  0.8 work.
- **The Mod API is unpublished.** It publishes in 0.8 under
  [`mod-api-release-policy.properties`](../../mod-api-release-policy.properties).
  The example mods are framework previews, not releases.
- **Experimental features.** The level editor, Stageplay, cross-game donation
  and expanded teams are experimental. Widescreen beyond 400 pixels is
  best-effort (528) or exploratory (800). Translations are machine drafts.
- **Platforms.** Native-package code-mod loading is an experimental VM facility.
  The native build recipe has bounded local Linux x64 evidence; Windows x64 and
  Apple Silicon native runtime and gameplay qualification are not recorded.
- **Time Attack network services** are retained under regression and qualified
  further in 0.8. The native two-player competition modes have internal
  groundwork but are not available from the menus.

Known defects are tracked in [known bugs](../status/known-bugs.md) and
[S3K known bugs](../status/s3k-known-bugs.md); deliberate departures are in
[known discrepancies](../status/known-discrepancies.md).

The project does not ship copyrighted game assets. Users supply dumps of games
they own, and runtime assets are loaded from those ROMs.
