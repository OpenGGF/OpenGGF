# Sitar Hero authored career story

## Scope and authoring decision

Origin: the 2026-10-07 authored world-tour refinement, based on
`1e89086fdfbb50b2ad6700ddf463dc252a422fb3`. The worker owns only
`sitarhero.story.CareerStory`, its dedicated packaged checks, and this artifact.
The existing full-version design owns shared interfaces, world IDs and delivery.

The 35 world introductions and three tour outros are original exchanges, rather
than a joke template filled with zone names. Each ROM tells a complete story.
The continuing bargain is explicit at each tour's opening: Sonic brings audiences
to free, open shows; Robotnik supplies genuinely worthwhile music while pursuing
a control scheme. Successful playing is respected, including playable Robotnik.
The scheme fails through offstage circuit decisions, without invalidating the
concert or making success a mistake by the player.

The user's request for more than surface character traits determines the beats.
Sonic makes room for small spectators, takes requests and changes his entrance
when friends make a better case. Tails wants to hear his own arrangement, enjoys
craft he did not invent, disagrees with improvisation that disrupts the band and
keeps a manual option for live decisions. Knuckles knows the island and makes
practical judgments; he accepts one credible perimeter-security proposal, then
traces the real wiring and removes its Emerald coupling when evidence contradicts
the proposal. Robotnik can repair, compose, listen, win a joke and earn applause;
his continuing ego and wish for control remain his own choices.

Robot performers use physical stage business and bounded display signals:
Silver Sonic starts with exact counts and learns to leave room for live answers;
Mecha Sonic offers a spare musical response rather than merely copying Sonic;
Egg Robo manages props, cables and scores with increasingly clear distinctions.
None is an interchangeable sarcastic human voice.

Each selected available performer contributes one authored local line immediately
before the closing beat. The contribution may support a quiet moment; it need not
be a punchline. A performer whose donor ROM is missing contributes no portrait or
line. A null performer returns the main exchange unchanged. These are cosmetic
variations, without ability, scoring, progression or plot branches. The scripts
are created on demand, with no static object collections.

The first Astra/Opus design suggestions for mutual advantage, varied warmth,
Robotnik's respected performances and an evidence-based Knuckles turn are adopted
as recorded in the parent design. Robotnik's supposed ownership of every stage,
involuntary touring, tragic motivation and a redemption arc are not used.

## Bounded final review

Astra and Opus reviewed actual Chemical Plant, Lava Reef and playable-Robotnik
finale exchanges. Their three material corrections are applied without another
general rewrite or review loop. Tails' selected switch contribution now offers
concrete confidence and fondness for Sonic's improvisation instead of repeating
a band-management maxim. Knuckles tersely acknowledges accepting the alarm
pretext, retains the decisive coupling removal, and permits its return somewhere
off his island. Robotnik still listens to the professional count, but no longer
nods graciously to the entire band; his solo continues while his takeover is
merely "postponed" in his own account. Sonic invites the big entrance instead of
delivering an audience-choice lesson.

Astra's selected-line proposals are used. Opus' alternative switch "clunk" joke
was considered but not chosen: the concrete plan to follow Sonic both warms the
existing disagreement and carries into the later airborne count. An extra
Robotnik excuse and another rehearsal rationalisation were not added; his active
reply is already present and his self-serving finale line preserves the ego.
The broader reviewed range, competence and warmth are retained. No new reviewer
approval requirement is inferred.
